How it works

Four steps, and the fourth is the one shops never do

01 · Listen

Read the chats

Every WhatsApp message a customer sends is tagged as it arrives: a restock they asked for, a delivery that went wrong, a party coming up. Evidence has to be quoted from something the customer said.

02 · Score

Find who is movable

An uplift model predicts the difference a message makes, not whether someone returns. Someone coming back anyway is a wasted discount. Someone gone for good is a wasted message.

03 · Write

Fill an approved template

WhatsApp forbids free text to open a conversation, so one of six approved sentences is chosen by the customer's strongest tag and its blanks filled. Invent a price and the message is thrown away, not patched.

04 · Prove

Keep a holdout

Some customers are deliberately not messaged. Without them you cannot tell a good message from a customer who was returning regardless, which is why most campaign dashboards measure nothing.

Honest state

Working, and not working

Working

  • Scoring a real shop from a purchase export, with a ranked list of who to message first
  • Tagging one message at a time, fast enough for a live webhook
  • Six approved templates, filled and checked, rejected rather than repaired
  • A reply bot that answers or hands to a person, and cannot be talked out of handing over
  • Campaign measurement with a holdout and a measured lift

Not working yet

  • No inbound webhook. MantaGO's documented API only sends. Until that is answered, nothing two-way runs for real
  • No recency feature. The training data exposes none, so every lapsed customer scores Lost Cause — visible on the x axis above, and a problem for a win-back tool
  • Sarcasm reads as satisfied. "Three days for a carton of eggs, very efficient service" is the one tag the test set still misses
  • Nothing sent to a real customer. No approved templates on a business number means broadcasts sit unsent
