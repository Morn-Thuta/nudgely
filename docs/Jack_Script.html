3 · How it works

4:40–5:40 · 140 words across both slides
Slide 6

"Three parts. First, an uplift model. It doesn't ask who might leave; it asks who a message actually changes. It runs on ten things any shop's receipts have. Second, an AI reads each conversation and keeps the details with the customer's exact words as evidence, then drafts the nudge from them. Third, every campaign keeps a no-message group, so the shop sees its own lift."

Q&A (5:00): your questions

Answer first in one sentence, then the trade-off. 30–45 seconds each. You take AI, data, the model, measurement and safety; Shareef takes product, flow and the business case. If you're unsure, the other adds one sentence, no more.

Why uplift and not a churn score?

"Churn says who might leave. Uplift says who a message actually changes. A regular like Hafiz comes anyway, and someone gone for good won't come back whatever you send. Messaging either wastes money. Only uplift skips both."

How accurate is the model?

"On customers it never saw, our top 30% caught 57% of the extra visits, against 30% at random, about 1.9 times better. It ranged from 38 to 85% across seven tests, because a single SMS only moves visits by about 0.75 of a point. So it's reliable for who to message first, not as an exact number per person, and every campaign measures itself anyway."

Your evidence is Russian grocery data.

"True, and we say so. Lenta ran a real randomised test on 687,000 shoppers, which is why we can learn what the message itself caused. That's exactly why we're asking for a pilot, not a sale."

What data does it use?

"The shop's own receipts and chats. The model needs only phone, date and amount, turned into ten numbers like how regular someone's visits are. No age, gender or children, on purpose. Phone numbers are never stored on the chat side; names are removed before the AI."

How do you measure retention?

"Every campaign holds back a random no-message group. We match the shop's sales to both groups over 30 days and compare return rates. The difference is the lift, shown with the range it could really be."

Does it learn from my shop?

"It starts from what it learned on public data. Every campaign records who was messaged, who was held back, and who came back. That's exactly what's needed to retrain on the shop's own customers once there are a few hundred."

When does it hand over to a person?

"Refunds and complaints, when someone types HUMAN, photos or voice notes, anything it can't verify like a price or stock that isn't in the shop's list, and if the daily AI budget runs out."

What if a service fails?

"The AI falls back to other models; if all of them fail, a person takes over. If WhatsApp is down nothing is sent, and the owner sees it."

Why this segment?

"Neighbourhood shops already run on WhatsApp but have no marketing team. Their customers are few enough that every wasted or annoying message matters."

Isn't this just SleekFlow or WATI?

"They send; we decide who and what, and prove it worked. We'd plug into them as the delivery layer."

Why not MantaGO?

"We started on MantaGO. Its API doesn't share incoming chat text, and its WhatsApp delivery didn't work on our account, so we built on WhatsApp's official API, which is what makes the AI conversation possible. Platforms like MantaGO are natural delivery partners."

What would a pilot need? · What does it cost?

"One shop, a WhatsApp business number, a four-week sales export, consent wording at signup, and a no-message group. It costs about $0.00025 per AI reply, plus WhatsApp's per-message fee."

Why gradient boosting, not deep learning?

"It's the standard winner on small tabular data like receipts. Neural uplift models need tens of thousands of outcomes, which a small shop won't have for a long time."
